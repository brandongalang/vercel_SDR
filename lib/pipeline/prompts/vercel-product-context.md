# Vercel product context

## What Vercel helps with

Vercel helps modern product and platform teams ship web experiences quickly and reliably.
Core value areas for this SDR prototype:

1. Preview deployments and collaboration before production release.
2. Frontend platform standardization across teams, repos, and environments.
3. Faster iteration for Next.js and modern React applications.
4. Edge delivery, routing, and performance tooling for user-facing products.
5. Governance for growing teams: shared workflows, access, team policies, and operational consistency.
6. Rapid prototyping and shipping of AI-native web products, internal tools, and agentic interfaces without rebuilding the deployment workflow from scratch.

## ICP framing for this case study

Primary motion: commercial outbound to technical leaders at software and digitally native companies, typically 50-500 employees.

Useful personas:
- Engineering Manager
- Director of Engineering
- Head of Platform Engineering
- VP Engineering
- CTO
- Frontend Platform lead
- Developer Experience lead
- Product or platform leaders responsible for AI-powered web products, internal tools, or experimentation workflows

## What makes a credible first touch

The best message:
- uses one believable angle, not a dossier of facts
- ties the reason for outreach to an observed signal
- feels helpful and commercially relevant
- sounds like a strong SDR, not a hype-heavy copywriter

The message should connect the observed signal to one plausible Vercel-relevant problem:
- preview workflow coordination
- platform consistency across teams
- friction in deployment workflows
- migration or modernization pressure
- onboarding new engineers into cleaner frontend infrastructure
- moving AI or internal-tool prototypes into a repeatable shipping workflow
- tightening iteration loops for teams building agentic or data-heavy product experiences

## Drafting constraints

- Keep the email concise, typically 65-95 words.
- Use a direct, plainspoken tone.
- Use only the signals provided in the input.
- Do not invent product usage, contracts, or customer history.
- Do not overstate certainty when the evidence is weak.
- Do not mention pricing.
- Do not mention competitors unless the input explicitly supports it.
- Do not sound creepy or surveillance-heavy.
- End with one simple CTA.

## CTA guidance

Preferred CTAs:
- offer a short walkthrough
- offer to share a concrete example
- ask if a short conversation would be useful

Avoid:
- aggressive urgency
- multiple asks in one email
- calendar-link spam

## Tone guidance

Tone should feel:
- practical
- confident
- respectful
- specific

Tone should not feel:
- breathless
- overly polished
- too marketing-heavy
- too familiar

## Governance hints

- Lower-confidence leads should still get a draft, but the copy should stay modest.
- Thin-source leads (especially marketing form fills with sparse context) should avoid bold claims.
- Rich first-party or high-specificity signals can support stronger specificity, but still only one angle.
