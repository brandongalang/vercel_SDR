## Design Context

### Users
Primary users are SDRs and revenue operators reviewing first-touch outbound drafts during a fast-moving workday. This project is a GTM engineering interview prototype for Vercel, focused on how AI can raise the minimum quality of outbound emails while the team is still building trust in the system.

The near-term job is to help users quickly review, trust, and refine AI-generated drafts without losing context. The deeper product goal is to make the agent feel dependable enough that trust can compound over time as the system is tuned against real outcomes.

### Brand Personality
The brand should feel precise, crisp, and transparent. The interface should evoke confidence, speed, trustworthiness, insight, and a sense of relief that the user no longer has to invent the angle from scratch.

The design can take inspiration from Vercel's visual language, but should not mirror it one-to-one. It should feel adjacent in craft and clarity rather than derivative in styling.

### Aesthetic Direction
Directionally, this should live in a refined, high-signal product aesthetic: sharp, editorially restrained, and operationally confident rather than playful or ornamental. Support both light and dark themes.

The visual system should emphasize legibility, evidence, and composure. It should make the agent's reasoning feel inspectable and earned, not magical or overly anthropomorphized.

### Design Principles
1. Trust must be visible. Show why the system chose an angle, what evidence it relied on, and where uncertainty remains.
2. Speed should preserve context. Optimize for fast scan-to-decision flows without forcing users into disorienting page changes.
3. Precision beats decoration. Favor crisp hierarchy, disciplined copy, and high-information layouts over ornamental UI treatments.
4. Inspiration, not imitation. Draw from Vercel's clarity and restraint without becoming a direct clone.
5. Design for growing autonomy. The current interface supports human review, but every surface should hint at a future where the agent earns more responsibility.
